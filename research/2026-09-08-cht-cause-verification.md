# CHT cause verification and contribution limits

Decision: retain CHT as a plausible humanitarian contribution target. The current
project record now includes a registry observation and a primary trial publication
beyond the steward's reach claims. These additions do not change any opportunity
gate or score. #10155 still requires maintainer confirmation and reproduction.

## Evidence reviewed

| Claim | Assessment | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cht-core` is a recognized Digital Public Good. | A: the [DPGA registry](https://www.digitalpublicgoods.net/r/community-health-toolkit) names the evaluated core repository and displays Verified status. | Assessment scope excludes unlisted extensions. Scale and SDG relevance are identified as self-reported. |
| A CHT-based service has been evaluated experimentally. | B: the [published randomized trial](https://www.jmir.org/2023/1/e42111) directly identifies CHT in its methods. | It tested a complete texting service, not the software's isolated effect. |
| The trial proves our proposed admin fix will improve health outcomes. | E: unsupported. | Different intervention and outcome; no measured link to #10155. |

The trial was unblinded and excluded people without their own phones. Connectivity,
language coverage, extra study support, and differential counseling limit
generalization. Its visit-count result does not establish equivalent reductions
in total staff time. Coauthors included Medic staff, so describe it as primary
published research, not an independent audit of the steward. No raw-data analysis
or replication was performed here.

Both sources were accessed 2026-09-08. A fresh request to the previously recorded
Medic scale article failed; its older claim and original 2026-09-06 access date
were preserved, not represented as newly verified.
